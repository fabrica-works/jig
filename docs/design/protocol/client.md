# The client's protocol

Provisional, 2026-10-10, revised 2026-10-12. What carries the contract
between a party's client and the engine (`../client/domain/contract.md`)
as bytes: the wire's records and their versions, the one connection a
client keeps, its exchanges and its stream, how a keyed request's answer
becomes the request table's envelope, reads, watches and a run's text,
signing in and the secrets it leaves, the small store's encoding, an
application's documents and requests on the same connection, and the
shells. Its two sides are components of the engine's protocol layer and
of the client's (`README.md`, section 5). What is still open is listed
in section 15.

## 1. In one page

- **One connection per client,** to the engine's one origin: HTTP for
  exchanges (each keyed request, each read, opening and closing a
  watch), and one stream of server-sent events for every delivery,
  tagged with its watch (`../client/domain/contract.md`, section 10).
- **Records from schemas, per owner.** `jig-protocol-client-wire` holds
  jig's requests, answers, reads, subjects and documents, generated by
  skein's generator, used by both ends. Each application's wire crate
  holds its own the same way. A record crosses whole, as the binary bytes
  of its schema: an exchange's body as they are, a stream event's data in
  base64.
- **One attempt per exchange.** The client's protocol layer makes one
  attempt per request, read or opening, and says how it ended. For a
  keyed request it makes the request table's envelope: *final* with the
  engine's answer decoded into its owner's vocabulary, *again*, *lost*,
  or *signed out* (`../client/domain/root.md`, section 4). Retrying is
  the table's and the domain's.
- **The stream paces the watches.** A delivery is taken when its event
  has been written to the client's connection; a client that reads slowly
  fills its connection, and the engine coalesces its watches' changes
  meanwhile. Nothing is acknowledged by hand, and the engine keeps no
  backlog.
- **The stream keeps the link.** It opens with a hello (the engine's
  versions, its heartbeat), carries a comment at each heartbeat while
  nothing else is sent, and its loss drops every watch on it, which the
  client domain reopens.
- **Signing in is each provider's exchange,** with the client's shell.
  jig ships one provider, the deployment's link; an application's
  providers are components of its protocol layer, to which the engine's
  side routes their sign-in routes. Signing in leaves a sign-in's secret:
  an HTTP-only cookie in a browser, a bearer token elsewhere. The engine
  keeps its digest, and hands the domain only the sign-in's number.
- **Versions per owner,** jig's and each application's; the engine serves
  a range of each, which its stream's hello states; a client outside a
  range shows that it is out of date and keeps what is pending.
- **The small store is the wire's records,** per owner, versioned, written
  by the shell before any request of the same step leaves.
- **Shells are small:** a browser's, the client compiled to WebAssembly
  over the platform's fetch; a native one over skein's HTTP client and
  server-sent events reader. Each maps its platform onto the same
  component.
- **On one person's machine** (`README.md`, section 4) the browser reaches
  the engine on a loopback address, in plaintext, and the person signs in
  by a link the engine prints at start (section 8).

## 2. Where it sits

```
client                                          engine
client root and view (../client/domain/)        engine's root (core, connectors)
  ▲ the client's vocabulary, per owner            ▲ the core's vocabulary, and connectors'
jig-protocol-client-link ◄── one connection ──► jig-protocol-engine-clients
  ▲ jig-protocol-client-wire, the application's   ▲ jig-protocol-client-wire, the application's
the shell: fetch, or skein's HTTP client        skein's HTTP server and server-sent events writer
  ▲                                               ▲
the platform's HTTP stack, TLS                  TLS server, or plaintext on loopback; io
```

- **`jig-protocol-engine-clients`** is a component of the engine's
  protocol layer: the HTTP server's exchanges for the client's routes, the
  clients' streams, the deployment's link, an application's providers'
  sign-in routes routed to them, sign-ins' secrets checked against their
  digests, the translation between the wire's records and the core's
  vocabulary, and the stream's pacing. An application's requests, reads
  and subjects are decoded by the application's wire, through the
  protocol layer's routing, and reach the engine's root typed.
- **`jig-protocol-client-link`** is a component of the client's protocol
  layer: each attempt's exchange, the stream and its watches, the
  envelopes, the small store's records encoded and decoded, the stream's
  hello judged against the client's versions, and the translation between
  the wire's records and the client's vocabulary. It is step code, built
  for the host and for wasm32, and gives its shell a method, a path,
  headers and a body, and takes back whole bodies, and the stream's body
  as it comes, which skein's server-sent events reader, inside the
  component, cuts into events, so every shell is as small as it can be
  (`../README.md`, section 10).
- **The shell** is the platform's: its HTTP stack, its storage, its
  clock, its entropy, and its sign-in navigation
  (`../client/domain/view.md`, section 11).

## 3. The wire

- **`jig-protocol-client-wire`,** a codec crate generated from schemas
  (skein's `codec.md`), holds jig's records for both ends: the asks of
  `../client/domain/contract.md`, 3.2, and their answers, with refusals'
  facts (section 4 of it); the reads of its section 6 and their pages; the
  subjects of 7.1 and their windows; the documents of 5.1; a run's text
  (7.3); the stream's hello and endings; and the small store's records
  (section 10).
- **The records are the contract's,** field for field: the joint world's
  translation (`../client/domain/testing.md`, section 5) is the map each
  end's translation follows, made bytes. Where the joint world finds the
  contract short, the contract changes first, and the wire follows.
- **Each application's wire crate** holds its own: its families' asks and
  their outcomes, its subjects, reads and documents, and its small store's
  records. It is generated the same way, and depends on no crate of
  jig's but `skein-codec`.
- **Translation at each end:** `jig-protocol-engine-clients` between the
  wire and the core's vocabulary; `jig-protocol-client-link` between the
  wire and `jig-client`'s; the application's protocol layer between its
  wire and its connectors' and client sides' vocabularies. Each is a
  module of small total functions, exhaustive both ways, as the channel's
  are (`channel.md`, section 3).
- **Documents are decoded whole,** into the client's types, and checked
  against the client's limits before they reach the domain: a document
  past its bytes, rows or texts is refused at decoding, as too large,
  never cut silently (`../client/domain/client.md`, section 16). The
  wire's generated worst cases price each document at the client's
  limits.
- **Turns cross as their bytes,** smith's, inside the documents that
  carry them, and are decoded by `jig-client-transcript` in the client's
  protocol layer (`../client/domain/client.md`, section 10); a turn
  that does not decode crosses up as such, never dropped.
- **No time crosses, only walls.** Documents carry wall times, which the
  store keeps (`../client/domain/contract.md`, 5.3); durations (a
  retention, a heartbeat) cross as durations.

## 4. The connection

- **One origin.** A browser's client is served by the engine from its
  origin (section 12), so its exchanges and its stream are same-origin;
  a native client and a service name the engine's address.
- **Exchanges** are POSTs to the client's routes, one record each way:
  a keyed request, a read, opening a watch, closing one, marking a
  delivery's stream gone. Each carries its sign-in's secret (section 8)
  and the version of the owner whose record it is (section 9).
- **The stream** is one GET per client, answered with server-sent events
  for as long as the client keeps it. Its first event is the hello:
  the stream's name, the versions the engine serves of jig's wire and of
  each application's, and the heartbeat. Every watch the client opens
  names its stream.
- **Heartbeats** are comments the engine writes on the stream whenever
  it has sent nothing for the heartbeat's span. The client's protocol
  layer tells each of the stream's watches it is alive at each
  heartbeat and each event; past twice the span with nothing read, it
  takes the stream as dropped.
- **A dropped stream** drops every watch on it, each told so, and the
  engine ends them as the client left. The client domain reopens them,
  and the stream with them, after its backoffs
  (`../client/domain/client.md`, 8.2).
- **HTTP/1.1,** over TLS, or in plaintext over loopback. A browser keeps
  one connection for the stream and opens others for exchanges as it
  needs, within its own bound; a native client the same, within the
  component's limits.

## 5. Keyed requests

- **One exchange per attempt.** The request table's send (its attempt,
  its key, its request) becomes one POST: the key, the request's owner
  and record, and the sign-in's secret. The answer, or its absence, is the
  attempt's one terminal.
- **The engine's side** checks the secret (section 8); hands the core the
  sign-in's number, the key and the request, decoded into the core's
  vocabulary; and, for an application's request, the digest of the
  record's bytes as they came (SHA-256), which tells a key sent again with
  another request apart (`../client/domain/contract.md`, 3.3). The
  record is canonical (skein's `codec.md`, section 1), so the same request
  always has the same digest.
- **The answer** is one record, of one of the contract's kinds
  (`../client/domain/contract.md`, section 4): done, with its outcome;
  refused, with its facts; busy; not ready; signed out. The engine's
  protocol layer makes it from whichever of the core's outputs answers
  the request: a party's reply, an application's outcome its connector
  kept, or a refusal before any child changed.
- **The client's envelope:** done and refused are *final*, with the record
  decoded into its owner's vocabulary: jig's by
  `jig-protocol-client-wire`, an application's by its own wire; busy and
  not ready are *again*; signed out, or a secret the engine does not know,
  is *signed out*; an exchange that failed, timed out or ended without a
  whole answer is *lost*. A final answer that does not decode is *lost*
  too, and reported: the engine has it, and sending the request again
  under its key fetches it again.
- **The attempt's deadline** is the client's, from its limits: past it the
  exchange is abandoned, and the attempt is *lost*.

## 6. Reads

- **One exchange per read,** answered once: the document, a page with its
  cursors, or why there is none (unknown, busy, signed out)
  (`../client/domain/contract.md`, section 6). A page's cursor is the
  engine's, opaque bytes the client gives back.
- **An application's read** names its scope and its connector's read, in
  the application's wire, and is answered with the application's
  document, or unknown, or not allowed.
- **Held until durable** on the engine's side, as every read's answer is
  (`../domain/engine.md`, 5.2); the exchange waits for it.

## 7. Watches

- **Opening a watch** is an exchange: the stream's name, the client's name
  for the watch, its subject and window. It is answered admitted, or
  refused (unknown, not allowed, busy, signed out); the first delivery
  follows on the stream. A subject an application's connector judges is
  admitted at the end of the step that takes it, and the connector's
  refusal, if it comes later, ends the watch with its reason
  (`../client/domain/contract.md`, 7.2). The engine names the party from the sign-in, so
  a subject of the party's own (their frame, their inbox, their
  conversations) names none.
- **A delivery** is one event on the stream: the watch's name, and the
  document, whole, in its owner's wire. The engine's side writes it when
  the core releases it (held until durable, `../domain/engine.md`,
  section 11), and tells the core it was taken once the event has gone to
  the connection. While the connection does not drain, nothing more is
  written for that stream, and the views coalesce what changes
  meanwhile. A client is never told it missed anything.
- **Ending:** an event naming the watch and why
  (`../client/domain/contract.md`, 7.2); or closing it by an exchange,
  answered once the engine has ended it. A stream that ends ends its
  watches.
- **A run's text** is a watch of the run: its deliveries carry the turn's
  name, the text after the offset the client has, and the offset it ends
  at (`../client/domain/contract.md`, 7.3). A watch opened, or opened
  again after its stream dropped, starts from the beginning of the turn
  being written, within its bound; the client keeps what it holds and
  takes what goes past it (`../client/domain/client.md`, section 10).
  These events leave without waiting for a commit, marked provisional.
  Only a run whose host gives its text, and the call it is making, has
  them (`channel.md`, 6.4); a run in an agent process has neither.

## 8. Signing in

- **jig ships one provider,** the deployment's link, for one person's
  machine (below). Services' credentials it issues itself, under the
  deployment as their provider (below).
- **An application's provider** (an OAuth or OpenID server, a company's
  directory) is a component of the application's protocol layer, with
  its settings in the application's configuration and its exchange its
  own. `jig-protocol-engine-clients` routes the provider's sign-in route,
  and the redirect back to the engine, to that component, through the
  protocol layer's routing, and takes back an identity (the provider's
  number and the subject, with a login and a name for display) or a
  refusal, which signs nobody in. For a browser: the shell navigates to
  the engine's sign-in route for the provider, the component sends the
  person to its server, and the server's redirect comes back to the
  engine, where the component reads who the party is and drops what the
  exchange returned (`../domain/people.md`, section 3).
- **The sign-in,** then: the core hears the identity and commits the
  sign-in, which gives it its number; the engine's side keeps the digest
  of a fresh secret under that number, in the store's secrets' records;
  and only then hands the client the secret, which carries the sign-in's
  number beside its random bytes, so the engine finds its digest by
  number (`README.md`, section 8; `store.md`, section 9).
  Secrets come from a cryptographic generator of the component's, seeded
  from the kernel at start. The core says each sign-in that ends or
  expires, and the component erases its digest then.
- **A browser's secret** is a cookie the engine sets on its origin:
  HTTP-only, secure, same-site strict, scoped to the client's routes. The
  client's domain and its WebAssembly never see it. Every exchange and
  the stream carry it; a state-changing exchange also carries a header
  only the client's own code sets, so a page of another origin cannot
  make one.
- **A native client's secret** is a bearer token, which its shell keeps
  where its platform keeps secrets, and sends with each exchange. It
  signs in by a device's flow, or by a browser's on the same machine,
  whichever the provider offers.
- **The deployment's own link,** on one person's machine: at start the
  engine prints a link to its sign-in route carrying a one-time secret,
  valid for a short while; opening it signs that person in as the
  deployment's first owner, and spends the secret. One link is live at a
  time: when a browser reaches the link provider's sign-in route
  without a live link's secret, the engine prints a fresh one, and the
  page it answers says to find it in the engine's output. A native
  client on the same machine signs in by the same link: it sends the
  link's secret to the sign-in route in an exchange, and takes back its
  bearer token in the answer. It is jig's
  one provider, `jig-protocol-engine-clients`' own, which an application
  enables by configuration (`configuration.md`, section 3), and only on
  a loopback listener.
- **A service's credential** is issued when an owner makes the service:
  the engine's protocol layer draws it, keeps its digest, and answers the
  owner's request with it once, never again. The service's shell holds
  it, and sends it as a bearer token.
- **Signed out:** a secret the engine does not know, or a sign-in ended or
  expired, is answered signed out on every exchange and ends the stream,
  and the client goes to sign in (`../client/domain/client.md`,
  section 12). A person signing out is an exchange that ends their
  sign-in, its digest erased, the cookie cleared: the shell's exchange,
  as signing in is (`../client/domain/client.md`, section 12).

## 9. Versions

- **Per owner:** jig's wire and each application's carry their own
  version; an exchange names the version of each owner's record it
  carries, and a stream event's record is in the version its watch was
  opened in.
- **The engine serves a range** of each owner's versions, which its
  stream's hello states (`../client/domain/contract.md`, section 8). To
  serve a version is to keep its generated codecs and their translations
  beside the current ones. Pre-release, each range is one version
  (`README.md`, section 7).
- **A client outside a range** sends nothing more of that owner, shows
  that it is out of date and how to update (`../client/domain/pages.md`,
  section 8), and keeps what is pending in its small store; updated, it
  sends what is pending under its keys.
- **A browser's client** is served by the engine that serves its wire, so
  it is in range unless the engine was updated under a page already open;
  reloading the page updates it.

## 10. The small store

- **Records per owner** (`../client/domain/client.md`, section 11): each
  child's own, and the request table's, which hold whole requests, jig's
  and the application's. Each is encoded with its owner's wire, with its
  owner's store version, and wrapped in a record naming its owner, as the
  client root wraps them.
- **Written before sends.** The shell writes a step's records, in order,
  before any of the step's exchanges leave; a write that fails stops the
  step's exchanges, whose owners are told they were not sent.
- **Read at start,** each record handed to its owner; one of a version its
  owner does not read is dropped, and its owner told, so a request kept
  across an update that changed its record's version is lost, not sent
  wrong. Pre-release, nothing is translated.
- **Where:** a browser tab's session storage, as text the shell encodes; a
  file a native client writes whole and syncs; a service's, a file it
  syncs before its requests leave (`../client/domain/root.md`,
  section 11).

## 11. An application's documents and requests

- **On the same connection and stream,** composed in each end's protocol
  layer: a keyed request names its owner, jig or the application's
  family; a read and a subject name theirs; a stream event's record is
  its watch's owner's.
- **The engine's side** decodes an application's record with the
  application's wire, through the protocol layer's routing, before the
  engine's root sees it; on the way out it encodes the connector's
  document and outcome the same way.
- **The client's side** decodes with the application's wire before the
  client root routes the value to its family (`../client/domain/root.md`,
  section 7).
- **`ops`'s wire** (`jig-ops-protocol-client-wire`): infrastructure's
  environment requests and their outcomes, observability's and
  infrastructure's subjects and documents (incidents, alerts,
  environments, panels).

## 12. Shells

- **The browser's** (`jig-shell-web`): the client root, its view and this
  component compiled to WebAssembly, with only the glue the toolchain
  makes; exchanges and the stream over the platform's fetch, the stream
  read as server-sent events by skein's reader in the component; session
  storage for the small store; the tree mapped to HTML elements with their
  roles (`../client/domain/view.md`, section 11).
- **Served by the engine:** the engine's protocol layer serves the client's
  files (its HTML, its WebAssembly, its glue, the application's
  drawings) from its origin, under a strict content security policy:
  scripts from the origin only, no inline script, WebAssembly compiled
  only from the module the origin serves (`'wasm-unsafe-eval'`, which
  compiling it needs), connections to the
  origin only.
- **The native one** (`jig-shell-native`): skein's HTTP client on
  skein's io, handing the stream's body to the component, whose reader
  cuts it into events, for Linux and for the worlds
  that make bytes real; with no view, it is a service's shell.

## 13. Limits and the worst case

- **The client's:** exchanges in flight, an exchange's deadline, the
  stream's heartbeat tolerance, a record's bytes on each side (from the
  wire's worst cases at the client's limits), the stream's event bytes.
- **The engine's:** clients' streams and their watches (the views' limits,
  `../domain/engine.md`, section 13), exchanges in flight per client and
  in all, a request's bytes, the output a stream may have queued before
  it stops being written, and the heartbeat's span.
- **The worst case** of each side is its HTTP machines', TLS's and the
  component's, at those limits.

## 14. Testing

Each within jig's suites' budgets (`../../development/workflow.md`).

- **Step tests** in `jig-protocol-client-wire` and each application's
  wire: generated, as skein's `codec.md`, section 6 has them.
- **The component's machine worlds:** each side against a scripted peer
  of bytes: answers of every kind and none, deliveries paced by a slow
  reader, a stream dropped mid-event, a hello out of range, signed out,
  records that do not decode, and, on the engine's side, a sign-in routed
  to a scripted provider, answered with an identity and with a refusal.
- **The protocol world,** `tests/client-protocol`: the client's world's
  stories (`../client/domain/testing.md`) run through bytes, the client
  root and the fake person over `jig-protocol-client-link` and the native
  shell's machines, against the engine's root and core over
  `jig-protocol-engine-clients` and skein's HTTP server, joined by
  in-memory streams cut at random. Its referee is the joint world's, and
  adds: every record crossed decodes to what was encoded; no secret
  reaches a domain; a slow stream never holds the engine up.
- **The browser tier:** the browser's shell over skein's browser kit
  (skein's `browser.md`), a person reading the page by role and name,
  against an engine in the same loop.

## 15. Open questions

- **Several tabs:** each tab a client with its own stream; whether tabs of
  one person share one stream through the platform's means.
- **Bundling parts** in a jig read's answer (`../client/domain/contract.md`,
  section 11).
- **A binary stream:** a streamed response body of the wire's bytes in
  place of server-sent events and base64, a third smaller, when browsers'
  streaming fetch serves every platform the client targets.
- **HTTP/2:** one connection for the stream and the exchanges, when
  skein's HTTP has it.
- **JSON records:** the same schemas written as JSON, for clients not
  built on skein, when the generator writes them (skein's `codec.md`,
  section 7).
- **A provider kind of jig's for OAuth or OpenID:** a generic provider,
  configured by its issuer and its client, which jig would ship beside
  the deployment's link, so an application whose people sign in through
  a standard server writes no provider of its own (section 8).

## 16. What it owes and is owed

- **The client domain** is owed nothing new: reads and watches answered
  signed out (`../client/domain/contract.md`, section 6), a reopened run's
  watch taken from its turn's beginning (`../client/domain/client.md`,
  section 10), and signing out as the shell's exchange
  (`../client/domain/client.md`, section 12), and a run's document with
  no text and no call, for a run whose host gives neither
  (`../client/domain/contract.md`, 7.3), are in its design.
- **An application's protocol layer** is owed the routing of its
  providers' sign-in routes and redirects to their components, and owes
  `jig-protocol-engine-clients` an identity or a refusal for each
  (section 8).
- **The engine's domain** is owed nothing new: sign-ins numbered by their
  commit, and a party ending theirs (`../domain/people.md`, section 3),
  watches and reads as `../domain/engine.md`, section 11 has them.
- **skein owes** nothing for one person's machine: its HTTP server and
  client, server-sent events reader and writer, and browser kit serve as
  they are; between hosts, TLS's server side (`README.md`, section 10).
